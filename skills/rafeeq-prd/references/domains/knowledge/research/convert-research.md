## [1] رؤية شاملة عن المسلم الجديد: دراسة استراتيجية علمية تعليمية ✅

- **Author/org:** «إعداد: الفريق العلمي بمركز أصول» (Osoul Center scientific team), Riyadh. The legal-deposit page lists it under جمعية الدعوة والإرشاد وتوعية الجاليات بالربوة. 186 pages.
- **Year:** first edition 1443 AH (PDF created Aug 2021).
- **URL:** https://books.islamway.net/1/A%20comprehensive%20vision%20of%20the%20new%20Muslim-book.pdf. I found no copy hosted on Osoul's own site.
- **Accessed:** downloaded the PDF and extracted the text. Read pp. 14–19, 40–71, 91–122 and 141–147.
- **Takeaways:**
  - It includes Osoul's own 2015 survey of 114 converts, interviewed face to face. 65% were women and 62% lived in the UK. After telling others about their conversion: 50% said their social life was affected, 48% lost a relationship with someone close, 10% thought about leaving Islam and 6% lost a job (p. 48).
  - **Practice problems (p. 54):** 66% found it hard to keep up worship. 54% received wrong religious information from Muslims, and 54% could not find answers to their doubts. About 50% each struggled to find trusted teachers, a mosque or reliable sources, or were confused about which group to follow. 30% could not find a mosque that spoke their language. A Muslim friend was the most-used and most useful source of help. The internet was used half as often and was less effective, which the authors attribute to conflicting voices (p. 68).
  - **Integration (pp. 51–52):** 43% found some Muslim circles hard to join. The reasons given were discrimination by Muslims (44%), pressure to adopt particular outward forms (42%) and culture shock (41%). 30% reported psychological problems such as depression.
  - **Gradualism is the core rule (pp. 92–97, 116–122):** strengthen faith and values first, then general knowledge, then details. Avoid disagreements in fiqh. There is no need to change a permissible name or clothing, and hard matters are delayed. Someone who cannot yet recite al-Fatiha has an approved alternative (p. 120). Converts are told not to issue fatwas themselves (p. 97).
  - **Follow-up (pp. 99–103):** the first week is the most critical. Models: a buddy for each convert, carers with 5–10 converts each, WhatsApp or Telegram groups of about 50 split by gender, and regular contact "even just to ask about feelings". Gulf workers (drivers, shepherds) often cannot attend centre programmes because of their hours and distance (p. 71).
  - **Recommendations (pp. 143–146):** Filipinos are the largest group of converts in Saudi Arabia. Avoid asking for personal information at first. E-learning should go beyond knowledge, include a glossary, follow a sequenced multimedia curriculum, and measure impact. A quoted imams' conference draft accepts the shahada in the convert's own language (p. 147).

## [2] Becoming Muslim: The Lives of American Converts to Islam ✅

- **Author/org:** Dr. Osman Umarji, Anna Birawi, Dr. Tamer Desouky, Thomas Simpkins, Sheikh Abdullah Oduro. Yaqeen Institute for Islamic Research.
- **Year:** published 9 January 2025. Data collected in 2021 through the LEMC survey (n = 618; the report says the sample is not representative).
- **URL:** https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/data/reports/becoming-muslim-lives-of-american-converts-to-islam (PDF: https://4713562.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/4713562/Datasets/Yaqeen%20Reports%20-%20Becoming%20Muslim.pdf)
- **Accessed:** downloaded the 203-page PDF. Read the executive summary and recommendations (pp. 2–9) and passages from chapter 3 and the community chapter.
- **Takeaways:**
  - Friends who support them and a connection to a mosque were the strongest predictors of overcoming life challenges. Muslim friendships and community connection were "by far the most common and consistent predictors of positive faith development". Programmes succeed "because their focus is on relationship-building, not only on teaching Islam formally."
  - **Doubts:** 23% doubt "sometimes" and 16% "quite often" or "very often". 23% said doubts had made them consider leaving Islam. The most common topics were gender rules and roles (44%), feeling that Islamic rules restrict freedom (37%) and intolerance among Muslims (37%). Bad behaviour by other Muslims is a leading source of anguish.
  - About three in four see God as near, helping and forgiving, which goes with more spiritual practice. About one in four see God as distant or "cop-like", which goes with less practice.
  - Nearly seven in ten told their parents within the first year, but the report says "there may even be no 'right time'". One example is a sister who has to hide her prayer and fasting. Relationships with parents usually dip at disclosure and recover over time.
  - 55% attend an online community outside the mosque. Online spaces "have their place but should never replace physical spaces". Mosques with convert-specific resources scored higher on belonging.
  - Converts need more than rules about the five pillars: spiritual guidance, family counselling, financial help, addiction support, mental-health referrals, and short guides to Arabic words used in sermons.

## [3] Religious and Social Empowerment of Muslim Converts: A Qualitative Case Study of Mualaf Center Indonesia Peduli in Medan ✅

- **Note:** MCIP is **Mualaf Center Indonesia Peduli**, not a Western organisation.
- **Author/org:** Mandasari and Salamuddin, Universitas Islam Negeri Sumatera Utara. *Borneo International Journal of Islamic Studies* 8(2), pp. 143–172.
- **Year:** 2026 (fieldwork 8–16 April 2026).
- **URL:** https://journal.uinsi.ac.id/index.php/bijis/article/view/13900 · DOI https://doi.org/10.21093/bijis.v8i2.13900
- **Accessed:** downloaded the full PDF. Read pp. 143–162.
- **Takeaways:**
  - Guidance has three stages: consultation before the shahada, help with the shahada itself, and continuous mentoring afterwards. The shahada "does not automatically complete the process of identity formation."
  - Religious and social support must go together. Teaching alone leaves converts isolated, and material help alone does not build identity. Support includes zakat and food aid, business capital and skills training, and psychological support through personal consultation.
  - Worship is taught by demonstration first and practice second, with errors in movements, recitations and sequence corrected. Learning the Qur'an starts with Arabic letters. Repeated sessions let converts revisit material.
  - Barriers: not being ready psychologically, attending irregularly, old habits, family rejection, problems with work and housing, and limited funding and staff.
  - Limitation: the beneficiary view comes from **one** convert. The authors present the findings as contextual, not generalisable.

## [4] Convert Programming: Key Tips for Building Effective, Sustainable Support ✅

- **Author/org:** The Mecca Center Convert Engagement Committee (Illinois, USA).
- **Year:** PDF dated November 2025.
- **URL:** https://meccacenter.org/mecca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Convert-Programming-Tips.pdf (linked from https://meccacenter.org/converts/)
- **Accessed:** downloaded and read the whole 5-page PDF, plus the programme page.
- **Takeaways:**
  - "The strongest and most effective convert programs are run and led by converts." It calls for peer-led programming and representation of different backgrounds.
  - Start small with one programme built on trust. Then "ask your participants what else they need" and add more slowly.
  - Balance two kinds of programme: converts-only spaces for sensitive Q&A, and integration with the wider community. Teach the community about converts' struggles.
  - Involve the imam or local scholar from the start, with a clearly defined role: teaching, answering questions, or religious oversight.
  - Consistency builds trust: the same schedule, place and facilitators. Collect feedback regularly and set SMART goals. It prefers in-person programmes and uses online formats "only when necessary".

## [5] New Muslim Mentorship (iERA) ✅

- **Author/org:** iERA (Islamic Education and Research Academy), online training platform.
- **Year:** course page posted 6 October 2020. The notes PDF is dated December 2020.
- **URL:** https://training.iera.org/courses/new-muslim-mentorship · notes: https://training.iera.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/New-Muslim-Mentorship-Course-Notes-Full.pdf
- **Accessed:** Cloudflare blocked the page to curl, so I read it through WebFetch. Downloaded and read all 31 pages of the course notes. The lesson videos sit behind a free login and were not viewed.
- **Takeaways:**
  - Four phases: Welcome, Spiritual, Teaching, Empowerment. "The first 48 hours are critical." Meet daily for at least two weeks to teach prayer and the basics, by video call if needed.
  - Explain every Arabic phrase used ("Alhamdulillah, wudu, salah… even Allah!"). Don't overload: "Even a 1% improvement daily is progress." Changing one's name, ghusl and circumcision should not be pressed on a new Muslim without wisdom.
  - The three-month aim is salah, the pillars of iman and Islam, and dua and dhikr: "give them what they need, not what they want."
  - Pitfalls: progressing *too fast* ("a journey, not a sprint") and learning from "non-vetted public forums". Mentors should set limits on their availability, avoid burnout, and refer case-by-case matters to scholars.
  - Mentoring steps: listen, empathise, empower. The case studies cover hiding Islam from parents and shame about past sins. Eid can be lonely.

## [7] تأليف المسلم الجديد ✅

- **Author/org:** د. عبدالله بن إبراهيم اللحيدان. Published on شبكة الألوكة (Alukah), section آفاق الشريعة. First printed in مجلة المستقبل, issue 165, Muharram 1426 AH / February 2005.
- **Year:** added to Alukah on 16 May 2012.
- **URL:** https://www.alukah.net/sharia/0/41068/
- **Accessed:** fetched and read the full article.
- **Takeaways:**
  - **Respect the convert's feelings.** Embracing Islam "ليس حدثًا صغيرًا في حياته", and the change is bigger for someone who had standing in their old community.
  - **Never shame a convert** for their past religion, sins, mistakes or parents. The article cites the story of a man who said: «كنت يهوديًّا وأسلمت، فصرت أعيَّر باليهودية، فلا تعيِّروني بها فأرجع إليها». It also cites ʿUmar rebuking those who mocked a convert's mistake.
  - **Preserve the convert's standing and give them a role:** «خيارهم في الجاهلية خيارهم في الإسلام إذا فقهوا».
  - Winning hearts with gifts (تأليف بالعطايا) is part of the Prophetic method.

## [8] النوازل العقدية للمسلمين الجدد ✅

- **Note:** this is a **journal article**, not a thesis or book. Emarefa gives the English title "The Contractual Insults of New Muslims", a poor translation.
- **Author/org:** د. زياد بن حمد العامر, associate professor of creed and contemporary ideologies, Majmaah University. *المجلة الدولية للدراسات الإسلامية المتخصصة* (Refaad, Jordan), vol. 1, issue 2, pp. 118–139.
- **Year:** 31 December 2018.
- **URL:** https://www.refaad.com/Journal/Article/786 · DOI 10.31559/sis2018.1.2.1 · full issue PDF: https://www.refaad.com/Files/SIS/SIS(1-2)%20FULL.pdf
- **Accessed:** downloaded the issue PDF and read the article (pp. 118–136): the plan, the rules, the cases and the conclusion.
- **Takeaways:**
  - **Principles for converts' cases:** necessity and need, weighing benefit against harm, telling permissible from impermissible disagreement, excuse for ignorance, coercion (family pressure), and judging each case individually. The author says that ignoring permissible disagreement has caused some converts to leave Islam.
  - The shahada in a non-Arabic language is valid if the person understands it. Repeating the Arabic words without understanding them does not make someone Muslim.
  - Someone who accepts Islam while still committing a sin, without claiming it is permitted, is Muslim. Rejecting a well-known ruling out of ignorance is excused because of that ignorance.
  - **Keeping one's Islam private:** the default is to declare it, but a convert may conceal their faith for a valid reason, such as family, social or security concerns (the Prophet told Abu Dharr to conceal his). Declaring it matters for practical rulings such as marriage and burial.
  - **Recommendations:** people who do da'wa to converts should be taught these creed rulings, and those without Sharia knowledge should not pronounce on them.

## What this changes in Rafeeq

Sources are cited by number. All content changes go through the Sharia reviewer.

1. **A human within 48 hours (PLT-01 → CMP-03, CMP-02) [1][2][5].** Offer a mentor right after onboarding and aim for the first check-in within 48 hours. Keep it optional, for privacy. Friendship and community predict healthy faith ([2]).
2. **Faith and values before detail (LRN-01, LRN-02, LRN-09) [1][5][8].** Order the path as Allah, His mercy and the shahada, then prayer, then the pillars. Delay disputed or heavy topics. Check LRN-09's scope against [5]'s three-month target.
3. **An early "what you don't have to change" lesson (LRN-09) [1][5][7].** Cover your name, your clothing and culture, and that your past is forgiven. Wording on ghusl and circumcision differs between scholars and goes to the reviewer.
4. **A route for anyone still learning al-Fatiha (LRN-01) [1].** Teach the alternative from [1] p. 120 while al-Fatiha is learned by listening, with no transliteration. Needs the reviewer's approval.
5. **Explain every Arabic term inline (KNW-03, all copy) [5][2][1].** Tapping a term shows its approved gloss in the user's language. Raise KNW-03 from P2.
6. **Approved answers ready for the most common doubts (KNW-01, KNW-04) [2][7].** Cache approved answers (rules §2.7) on gender roles, freedom and rules, and Muslims behaving badly, and say that individual Muslims don't represent the faith. Add these topics to the KNW-04 test set.
7. **Conversion and creed cases go to a human (KNW-01 routing) [8][1].** Questions such as "was my shahada valid?", "am I Muslim if I still…" or "must I tell anyone?" are C/D level and need a referral, never a model answer. [8] shows they depend on the person's circumstances.
8. **Privacy is legitimate (PLT-05, PLT-02, CMP-03) [8][2][1][5].** Present keeping one's Islam private as acceptable, never as failure. Keep neutral mode easy to find and ask for no personal data at sign-up. Give mentors a listening script for "should I tell my parents?", not a ruling.
9. **Demonstrate, then practise (LRN-03, KNW-08) [3][1].** Watch or listen first, then practise with step-level correction. Start the Qur'an from the letters. Make lessons easy to revisit.
10. **Pace against burnout as well as dropout (MOT-02, LRN-07) [5][2].** The learning coach should suggest review when someone rushes, and celebrate small steady progress. This fits the pausing streak and [2]'s finding that faith grows with time.
11. **Convert-led small groups (CMP-05, MOT-06) [4][1].** Single-gender groups that share a language, ideally led by a trained convert, with a gentle bridge to the local mosque. Show group progress collectively only.
12. **Reach workers who can't attend centres (PLT-03, LRN-03) [1][2].** Gulf workers and Filipinos, the largest convert group in Saudi Arabia ([1] pp. 71, 143), need full Tagalog, lessons of a few minutes, offline tolerance, and a list of local centres that keeps location on the device. Rafeeq should lead to the mosque, not replace it ([2]).
13. **Non-religious help categories (CMP-01, CMP-02) [1][2][3][5].** Add family pressure, job or housing loss, money and feeling low to the help menu. Mentors refer to the right service and keep set availability limits ([5]).
14. **Discover: hope and real stories; copy with dignity (KNW-06, KNW-07, UX copy) [1][2][6][7].** Add approved stories of converts and of Companions who were mentored, with no images. Daily cards should stress Allah's nearness and mercy, which [2] links to practice. Copy should never shame the user's past ([7]) and can say "no question too basic" ([6]).

## Gaps and cautions

- Most of the quantitative evidence comes from Western samples. [1] is mainly UK-based (n = 114, about two-thirds women) and [2] is US-based (n = 618, not representative). Neither describes Filipino or other Gulf converts directly. Local user research is still needed (`docs/agents/research/04`).
- [3] rests on administrators plus one convert, so treat it as illustrative.
- Not read: [2] beyond the summary and selected chapters; [5]'s lesson videos and its 13-week toolkit; the related articles linked from [7].
