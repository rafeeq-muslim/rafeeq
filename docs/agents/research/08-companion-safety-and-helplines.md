## 2. Same-gender private conversation

- IslamQA 34841: private chats between men and non-mahram women are not allowed because they lead to temptation; it quotes Ibn Jibreen: «لا يجوز لأي إنسان أن يراسل امرأة أجنبية عنه؛ لما في ذلك من فتنة» [S1].
- Osool's follow-up model splits groups by gender [C1 p.102]; the domain's fixed rules already require single-gender groups.
- Consequence: help requests, the mentor thread and groups are same-gender. A visitor is asked once, «أخ أم أخت؟».
- **Exception for danger** (proposed; CMP-07 was cancelled 2026-10-06): the first available person answers, whatever their gender, because a life or safety is at stake. ⚠️ For the Sharia reviewer to confirm.

## 3. What a mentor does and does not do

- Mentors set limits on their availability, avoid burnout, and refer case-by-case matters to scholars [C5].
- Conversion and creed cases («هل صحّت شهادتي؟»، «هل أنا مسلم وأنا…») depend on the person's circumstances; those without Sharia knowledge should not pronounce on them [C8].
- Practitioner designs cap a mentor at 5–10 converts [C1 p.102]. The first 48 hours matter most [C5].

## 4. Private notes and doubts (CMP-06)

- 23% of US converts doubt "sometimes" and 16% "often"; 23% considered leaving because of doubts [C2].
- 54% could not find answers to their doubts [C1 p.54].
- "There is no question too small or too basic" [C6]: a copy line that is not a Sharia statement.
- `rules.md` §1.2 and §4: collect only what is needed; never draw religious or sensitive inferences about the user. So the notebook stays on the device and no AI reads it.
