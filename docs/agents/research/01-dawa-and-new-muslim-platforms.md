# Benchmark: da'wa, dialogue and new-Muslim support platforms

Prepared 2026-10-04 for the Rafeeq (رفيق) hackathon team. Citations are [S#] and map to the Source list (section 7).

**Method and limits.**
- Pages were fetched through a tool that summarises with a small model. Every number below was read from the cited page, but verify before putting any figure on a slide.
- Almost all conversion counts are **self-reported by the organisation or ministry and unaudited**. Live counters were read on 2026-10-04.
- I could not read Reddit (r/converts, r/islam and r/MuslimLounge are blocked to my tools), so no forum evidence is included.
- I could not find or verify: the "هادي" bot from جمعية الروضة, a platform called "Bayan al-Islam", or "The Revert Project". Searching "هادي" on the Rawdah site and the Center of Excellence for Da'wa AI page returned nothing [S51][S53].
- Google Play pages did not load, so ratings are from the App Store only.
- The search budget ran out before I could cover the Canadian or Australian programmes in depth, or Saudi new-Muslim follow-up platforms (the Cooperative Offices' "المسلم الجديد" programmes).

## 1. Executive summary

- **The Gulf funnel is huge at the "shahada" end and thin at the "first year" end.** Reported scale:
  - Saudi Ministry of Islamic Affairs: 347,646 converts for 2019-2023, including 163,319 in 2023 [S11].
  - Dubai: 5,511 in H1 2026 [S16].
  - Kuwait: 2,800 in 2025 [S17].
  - Rokn Al-Hiwar (ركن الحوار): 291K via online dialogue [S1].
  - Chat & Decide: 68,763 cases in about 3.8 years [S3].

  Follow-up is mostly classes, content libraries, certificates and visits, not a structured, measured first-year journey.
- **Only two Gulf-linked platforms publish anything funnel-like.**
  - Rokn Al-Hiwar claims 160K "New Muslim Academy" students against 291K new Muslims (about 55%, enrolment rather than completion) [S1].
  - Chat & Decide's dashboard shows 43,717 "in education" and 25,751 "graduates" out of 68,763 cases, with "graduate" undefined [S3].
  - Nobody publishes 30, 90 or 365-day retention. Rafeeq can own that metric.
- **Western models are human-led and small.**
  - WhyIslam hands each new Muslim's contact details to a local volunteer mentor [S21].
  - The UK Islamic Foundation's New Muslims Project reaches about 100 people a year [S24].
  - iERA offers a free mentor-training course [S23].
  - Local groups such as Revert2Reality and Brisbane Muslim Fellowship are coffee, halaqa and Sunday-session models [S27][S28].
- **The "most converts leave" figures are not evidence.**
  - The viral "7 in 10" and "75%" claims trace to one imam's observation and a cited researcher, and the author says the exact numbers are hard to come by [S33].
  - Pew does not track people who converted in and later left [S34].
  - The first large UK study in over a decade (Ayaan Institute) launched in April 2026, with findings due later in 2026 [S26].
  - What is well documented, qualitatively, is **isolation after the welcome fades, family hostility, culture mistaken for religion, and little tailored guidance** [S31][S32][S36].
- **App competition is fragmented.**
  - Prayer-learning apps are well rated (Namaz App 4.9 from 15K+ reviews [S40]) but single-skill.
  - New-Muslim-specific apps are tiny: Reverts Guide has 1 rating [S43], Easy Islam 9 [S46], Kuwait's New Muslims' App 3 [S44].
  - A "Duolingo for Islam" app exists (Jibreel, 50K+ users claimed, English-only, Hanafi, deliberately no AI) [S42].
  - No product found combines a learning path, source-grounded AI, human mentors and many languages.
- **AI practice is converging on what Rafeeq proposes.**
  - A 2025 survey counts 20+ Islamic LLM chatbots and recommends citation transparency, no unsupervised fatwas, and human review [S48].
  - Dubai's Virtual Ifta launched in 2019 limited to about 205 prayer questions [S50].
  - Muslim AI Companion says it issues no rulings [S49].
  - Jibreel avoids AI because of hallucination risk [S42].
- **Trust and privacy matter for this audience.**
  - Muslim Pro was reported in 2020 to feed location data to a broker that supplied US military contractors; the company denied selling data [S41].
  - New-Muslim app Revertly ships a "privacy mode" that disguises its icon [S45], which suggests users who hide their faith from family or employers.

## 3. Notable platforms in more detail

**Rokn Al-Hiwar (Saudi).**
- It is the largest online da'wa dialogue operation I found.
- Its 2018 press statement (campaign "A Muslim Every Hour") gave 34,479 total converts and 488,269 people who had held a direct dialogue [S2]. My arithmetic, not theirs: about 7% of dialogue participants made shahada.
- The current site shows 291K new Muslims and 160K New Muslim Academy students [S1].
- The implied roughly 8x growth in eight years is unexplained, so treat it as a claim.
- The model is dialogue first, then a content academy, with an intent to "ensure continued education" [S1].
- Visible gaps: no completion data, and no explicit mentor handoff.

**Chat & Decide.**
- Its public dashboard is the most transparent funnel I found: total cases, "in education", "graduates", gender split (men 50,746; women 18,005), and top countries [S3].
- The country mix is a market signal. The Philippines is 20% (13,857), then Mozambique (7,769) and DR Congo (7,630) [S3].
- Caveats: "in education" plus "graduates" (69,468) exceeds total cases (68,763) by 705, so the definitions are unclear. The page also does not say what "graduate" means [S3].

**Dubai IACAD.**
- Its services are the most complete state package: certification, consultations, financial help via the Abu Dhabi Zakat Fund, marriage facilitation, and nine partner centres [S14].
- Counts: 852 in Q1 2020 and 838 in Q1 2019 [S15]; 5,511 in H1 2026 [S16].
- Rough, my own arithmetic: H1 2026 is about 3x the Q1 2020 run-rate (852/quarter, about 1,700 per half-year).

**Kuwait IPC.**
- Its 2025 report is the richest activity dataset: 775 courses, 7,210 students, 2,767 field visits ("Balagh"), 1,212 family visits ("Bait al-Aila"), and 4 Umrah trips for new Muslims [S17].
- In 2019 it ran classes on converts' days off and gave Zakat House aid for special cases [S18].
- The format is expat-worker-aware, but it depends on physical attendance.

**Qatar Guests Centre.**
- Its design is the closest to a formal curriculum: initial lessons, then misconceptions and Quran, then a **three-level, nine-month certificate**, plus follow-up with Islamic centres in converts' home countries [S19].
- The figures are from 2015.

**WhyIslam (ICNA).**
- It has a clear handoff chain: hotline, shahada, then contact details passed to local volunteers for mentoring [S21].
- At the ICNA 2019 convention, revert Nahela Morales asked "What happened to those shahadahs?" and co-founded EMBRACE for long-term support [S22].

**iERA and New Muslim Academy.**
- They are the nearest to Rafeeq's learning path. iERA offers bite-sized video lessons, a 14-week webinar series, worksheets, quizzes, mentors and retreats [S23].
- NMA adds gender-segregated peer groups with instructors, and runs free [S9][S9b].
- Neither publishes user numbers or completion rates.

**Jibreel and Muslim Pro (apps).**
- Jibreel proves demand for a Duolingo-style fard 'ayn path (50K+ users claimed) [S42].
- It is English-only and Hanafi-only, and it avoids AI for hallucination reasons.
- Muslim Pro dominates utility use (600K iOS reviews) and has moved into AI via AiDeen, but its ad load and privacy history are recurring complaints [S41].

## 4. What converts say they need

All quotes are from the cited sources.

- **The welcome fades.**
  - Lahmar (Bridge Institute, Dec 2018) found communities "welcome new converts warmly, but leave them to their own loneliness and navigation after the ceremony phase fades away". A UK convert wrote: "in the beginning, some reverts may have lots of attention... as time goes by things get lonelier and harder" [S31]. The evidence is qualitative, from a small PhD sample.
  - Morales (ICNA 2019): "It was the loneliest day of my life" (first Eid) [S22].
  - Monique Hassan: "most of us spend our Eids alone" [S32 Traversing Tradition].
- **Companionship over materials.** The "shahada boxes" critique argues for one-to-one companionship [S32]. Revert2Reality's founder starts with "coffee and conversation before education" [S27].
- **Tailored rulings and a distinction between culture and religion.**
  - Converts "seek help from the internet" when local scholarship is thin, and generic rulings can make situations "more difficult and stressful" [S31].
  - Faith Matters (2010) found 64% sought help online [S31].
  - Corbin (2016): "being Muslim is not synonymous with being Arab" [S37]. Her piece also calls for gradual learning, not rule overload [S37].
- **Family conflict.** Faith Matters 2010 (via secondary sources) reports about half struggled with acceptance by local Muslims and two-thirds faced negative family attitudes [S32]. The mosque often failed to help when family or friends were hostile [S32 ICLRS].
- **Practical support.** Yaqeen's Sheikh Abdullah Oduro (Ramadan 2022) names three needs: financial help, time and mentorship, and sharing of skills [S35].
- **First-year pacing.** Yaqeen's convert hub is built around the "first Ramadan" and the Five Pillars [S52].
- **Community gaps in the West.** In 2017, only a handful of the 80+ Bay Area mosques ran formal convert support classes [S36]. A convert said: "I just have no one to talk to who would not judge me" [S36].
- **Gaps in provision (UK, 2026).** Researcher Yahya Birt says support is inconsistent by region and materials are built for children, not adult learners [S26].
- **Migrant-worker converts (the Rafeeq core audience).**
  - In a Philippine study of Balik-Islam (converts), about 17% attributed conversion to work in the Middle East and about 18% to a preacher. The authors say "conversion is not a one-off thing" and call for transition and integration support [S38].
  - Returning OFWs often became da'wa leaders [S38].
  - Ethnography of South Asian domestic workers in Kuwait describes conversion as an everyday, ongoing process shaped by household life [S39].

**Gap in evidence.** I found no first-person Reddit or forum material (blocked). Rafeeq should run its own short interviews with converts in the Gulf, especially in Tagalog, Sinhala and Swahili-speaking groups.

## 6. Implications for Rafeeq

**Adopt**
1. **Start the journey on day 0 and pace it by the first year.** Use bite-sized lessons (Jibreel and Revertly use 5-minute and 23-lesson formats [S42][S45]), a 14-week-style core (iERA [S23]) and milestones such as week 1, first Ramadan, month 9. Qatar's 9-month, 3-level certificate is a Gulf precedent [S19].
2. **A real human handoff.** Copy WhyIslam's "pass contact to a local volunteer mentor" step [S21]. Use iERA's free mentor-training material as a baseline [S23b]. Add gender-segregated small groups (NMA [S9b]).
3. **Language-first.** Prioritise the languages the data points to: Filipino/Tagalog (20% of Chat & Decide cases [S3]; Kuwait uses Tagalog, Hindi, Sinhala [S18]), then Portuguese and French/Lingala/Swahili (Mozambique and DR Congo [S3]), plus Hindi/Urdu, Sinhala and Bengali, which I infer from Gulf workforce patterns.
4. **Take the handoff from dialogue platforms.** Chat & Decide has 43,717 people "in education" and Rokn Al-Hiwar 160K students [S1][S3]. Position Rafeeq as the destination after the chat, not a rival. A formal partnership is an open question, not confirmed.
5. **Privacy mode and no ad tracking.** Revertly's disguise feature [S45] and the Muslim Pro affair [S41] suggest discreet notifications, optional app-icon disguise and a no-selling-data pledge.
6. **Source-grounded AI that cites and refers.** This matches the survey's recommendations (scriptural fidelity, citation transparency, sectarian disclosure, human review) [S48]. Add a visible "ask a human mentor" path, as Jibreel plans ("Ask a Sheikh", 48h) [S42].

**Avoid**
- A content library with no path. islamhouse-style and Osool-style repositories already exist at scale [S7][S8], and dawa.center lists 9,517 files [S10].
- Rule overload and guilt mechanics. Converts ask for gradual learning [S37]; a competitor blog advocates "motivation without pressure" (note it is marketing) [S55].
- Fatwa-style AI answers, or silently picking one madhab. Namaz App and Jibreel are Hanafi [S40][S42]; Rafeeq should disclose and let users choose.
- Quoting the "7 in 10 leave" statistic in the pitch. It is anecdotal [S33].
- Telling users their data is private while using ad SDKs (see Muslim Pro [S41]).

**Differentiate**
- **First-year coverage plus measurement.** No competitor publishes completion or retention. Publish enrolment, completion, and 30/90/365-day return rates, with Rafeeq's own definitions.
- **Mentor plus AI plus path plus tools in one app, in many languages.** The existing apps each do one of these [S40][S42][S43].
- **Designed for isolation and family friction.** Include modules on family, culture-versus-religion, first Eid and first Ramadan (the pain points in [S22][S31]).
- **Low-friction channels for workers.** The Gulf programmes already use WhatsApp (Qatar [S19]; Dubai certificates [S15]) and in-person days off (Kuwait [S18]). Support WhatsApp reminders and offline lessons.
- **Gulf-aware context.** Include shahada documentation help (Kuwait requires a Ministry visit [S18]), employer or sponsor context, and returning-home continuity (Qatar's follow-up with centres abroad [S19]; Philippine returnees [S38]).
